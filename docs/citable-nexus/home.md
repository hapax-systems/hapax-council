# Hapax

Research and engineering on human-agent work, authority, evidence and consent.
For dependable work with AI over time: how intentions survive a handoff, how
actions remain within permission, how contributions are represented, and how
anyone can check what actually happened.

One person independently operates Hapax with AI agents contributing to research,
implementation and writing. Public findings include source code, research notes
and examples with explicit limits; individual publications explain contributions
and review status. A project byline does not mean every named participant
performed the same work. This is not a finished answer to the wider questions
about AI.

## The Questions Behind The Work

What makes assistance genuinely useful, including for someone who needs it
every day? How can a person retain judgment, change direction or refuse?
What should a record of human and agent contributions preserve, and what
should remain private? When does a useful tool add work, dependence or risk
somewhere else?

These questions need different kinds of evidence. A correct software check does
not settle questions about training rights, working conditions, compensation,
environmental costs or institutional power. A real concern about those matters
does not require denying that a particular tool can help.

## Commitments

Distinguish a person's direction from a machine's interpretation. Do not turn
access to information into permission to act. Make useful evidence available
without requiring the disclosure of private lives. Give claims the scope their
evidence supports, including when a result disappoints or needs correction.

These are commitments governing the work, not a claim that every implementation
already fulfills them. Public results must describe the checks actually made
and the questions still open.

## About The Work

The aim is useful, transferable work and a sustainable business built on that
value. Commercial interests should be visible. Payment must not purchase a
favorable finding, an endorsement, or control over a public standard.
